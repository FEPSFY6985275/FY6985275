Abstract

The widespread adoption of Internet of Things (IoT) devices creates significant cybersecurity challenges, as connected devices are vulnerable to unauthorised access, data breaches and cyberattacks. Artificial Intelligence (AI) offers a potential solution by analysing device activity and identifying suspicious behaviour. This article explores how AI detects cyber threats, examines common IoT vulnerabilities and evaluates the advantages and limitations of AI-based security. It also considers whether lightweight AI can protect devices with limited processing power and energy resources.

Introduction:The Growing Need for IoT Security

IoT refers to a network of physical objects embedded with sensors, software, and network technology. Examples include smart home devices, vehicles and industrial machinery. Although these technologies improve efficiency and convenience, their widespread use creates more opportunities for cyberattacks. One study reports that over 70% of IoT devices are vulnerable to cyberattacks, highlighting the need for stronger security (Intizar et al., 2026). As IoT devices become more common, protecting them is increasingly important. One solution is that AI can analyse device activity in real time and detect unusual patterns that may indicate malicious behaviour. This raises an important question: Can AI effectively improve IoT security while maintaining protection, accuracy, and system performance?

Common Vulnerabilities in IoT systems

IoT devices are often vulnerable to cyberattacks due to weaknesses in their design, software and security controls. Common issues include weak passwords, outdated firmware, delayed security updates and insecure communication channels. Cybercriminals can exploit these vulnerabilities to gain unauthorised access, steal sensitive information, or control connected devices (Kareem et al., 2025). For example, the 2016 Mirai botnet attack demonstrated how vulnerable IoT devices could be combined to launch large-scale Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks which flood websites with traffic, preventing legitimate users from accessing them. Other consequences of IoT attacks include financial losses, reputational damage and privacy breaches. These vulnerabilities highlight the need for security solutions that identify threats and protect connected devices and networks. (Intizar et al., 2026; Kareem et al., 2025).

How AI identifies Cyber Threats

Machine learning (ML) can identify cyber threats by analysing network traffic and device behaviour. It learns normal activity patterns and detects unusual behaviour, unlike traditional security methods that rely on detecting known attack patterns. Anomaly detection is a common ML technique in AI-based cybersecurity. A survey of AI- driven anomaly detection in IoT and sensor networks found that most existing research currently focuses on identifying malicious activity. It also reported that the GTA model achieved F1 scores of up to 0.91 on benchmark datasets, including SWaT and MSLS. The F1 score is a performance metric that considers how accurately a model identifies threats. Although these results are promising, benchmark datasets may not reflect how accurately a model performs in real-world IoT environments. Different devices and network conditions could affect how well a model performs (DeMedeiros et al. 2023).

The Potential of AI-Driven Protection

An advantage of AI security is its ability to analyse large amounts of data quickly and spot suspicious activity, helping security teams monitor connected devices and allowing quicker threat detection. Researchers have explored graph-based methods, which examine device connections to identify coordinated attacks, while federated learning and explainable AI aim to improve AI training and understanding (Alzahrani et al., 2026). Another development is lightweight AI, which is designed to improve security while using minimal computing power. Research into Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) security examines a TinyML-based intrusion detection system that operates locally on resource-constrained devices, without depending on cloud connectivity (Mostafavi et al., 2026). This suggests AI systems can work directly on IoT devices, making it more practical in certain environments.

Limitations of AI in IoT Security

Despite its benefits, AI security has several limitations. Models trained on limited datasets may struggle to recognise unfamiliar attacks. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) can create additional training data, but this does not guarantee real-world accuracy (Alzahrani et al., 2026). Moreover, many IoT devices have limited battery life and processing power, making complex AI models difficult to run. Privacy is also a concern because monitoring device activity can expose sensitive information. Therefore, AI systems must be efficient, reliable and secure to protect IoT networks effectively.

Conclusion: The Future of IoT Security

Overall, AI can strengthen IoT security by detecting suspicious activity. Research on anomaly detection and lightweight AI shows promising results, especially for devices with limited computing power (DeMedeiros et al., 2023; Mostafavi et al., 2026). However, data quality, device limitations and privacy challenges remain. Future research should focus on developing more efficient and reliable models. AI should also work alongside strong authentication, encryption and regular software updates (Intizar et al., 2026; Kareem et al., 2025). Ultimately, AI can improve IoT security, but it cannot prevent every cyberattack alone and is not a complete solution.
